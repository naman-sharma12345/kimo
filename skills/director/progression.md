# Progression, emotional rhythm and physical contracts

The second research pass adds twelve distinct sources. Their ideas are synthesized,
not installed. Provider defaults, dramatic quotas and named styles are not rules
for Kimo. A model guide is a lead to official capability verification, not proof.

## Choose the structure that fits
A narrative beat changes a goal, an obstacle, information or a relationship. Record
cause -> visible action -> consequence. The next scene receives that consequence,
not a reset to the same pose. A commercial can use need -> encounter -> proof ->
payoff; a music video can use anticipation -> release -> aftermath. These are
editable outlines, never a mandatory preset or a fixed time percentage.

Non-narrative films are valid. A performance, texture study or abstract sequence
can progress through scale, density, palette or rhythm instead of invented conflict.
State that intention. A quiet environmental shot may carry memory or expectation;
it does not need a fake plot event to justify its duration.

## Emotional rhythm
Write the entering and leaving emotional state, then express the change through
blocking, gaze, breath, sound distance or a small physical gesture. Do not prescribe
five simultaneous facial movements. Let an action have anticipation, contact and
settling time. Cutting faster is not the same as rising stakes. Leave a deliberate
low-density hold around a reveal, mouth-sync, product identity or difficult gesture.

Design phrase-scale contrasts: held/active, close/wide, quiet/loud, empty/populated.
Use a density map to notice constant maximum stimulation, not enforce a quota.
Tag each cut's reason and eye-trace handoff. Musical hits offer candidate cut points;
verify them against audio and favor the story/performance when the two compete.

## Motifs and color
Assign a motif a meaning and track its changed appearances. A prop, reflection,
color or sound should acquire meaning, not just recur identically. Record a payoff
or an intentionally unresolved ending. Protect product color and skin; change the
palette with an explicit scene reason rather than a global wash. A warm practical
may signal a relationship, but "warm = happy" is not a universal law.

## Lighting contract
Record source, direction, softness, temperature description, shadow behavior,
atmospheric interaction, reflection behavior and any transition cause. Separate
the light's existence from its effect on rain/glass/cloth. A moving lamp needs a
path; a flash needs a trigger. No vague "luxury light". Stable lights stay stable
across continuations unless an observed action changes them. No engine support is
implied by describing a light in a prompt.

## Optics contract
Record perspective, focus target, depth of field, focal length when relevant,
subject distance and shutter impression. Pair numeric lens anchors with visible
behavior. Do not infer perspective from focal length alone: camera distance
matters. A focus pull moves attention, not the camera; a digital zoom changes a
flat plate, not the world. Optical compensation or a dolly-zoom is an explicit
compound technique, not an excuse to stack unrelated camera verbs.

## Movement contract
Record mount, start/end position, direction, speed, acceleration, subject relation
and rationale. Preserve the observed camera phase at a continuation boundary.
`pedestal`, `leading_track`, `whip_pan` and `body_mount` extend the vocabulary, but
none is an automatic engine preset. A handheld or fast pan needs bounded readable
motion. Complex moves require a staged path and an inspected output, not hype.

## References and changes
Give references specific roles: identity, first frame, last frame, location, style,
camera motion, blocking or audio. A movement reference must not silently transfer
its performer, logo or room. Record intended transfers and exclusions. Never claim
an enhancer inspected images when only metadata was supplied. Protect user-locked
choices through prompt revisions. Keep the enhancing model separate from the
media-generation target and account permissions.

## Source conflicts deliberately rejected
- No blanket "always start from an image" ban on text-to-video. Use a checked
  keyframe when composition/continuity needs it and capability allows it.
- No fixed environment/performer ratio or generic effect-density target.
- No global ban on emotion words in human planning; production prompts must
  express them in visible/audible facts.
- No automatic provider choice, paid retry, repair or media upload.
- No bundled cinematic look, teal-orange grade or named-director imitation.
- A source's platform safety claim or model support table is not verification.
